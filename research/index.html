Research

I connect computational design with experiments to build DNA structures, from finite objects to open crystal lattices. My broader aim is to make programmable assembly a practical route to functional materials.

  1. Building 3D structures from DNA cubes

    Inverse design assigns interaction rules to a minimal, target-specific set of DNA-origami cubes, building finite objects and extended lattices while accounting for competing assemblies.

    Science Robotics · Accepted · Preprint↗ — Universal 3D Nanoscale Assembly with DNA Polycubes (opens in a new tab)

    Co-first and co-corresponding author

  2. A DNA-origami pyrochlore lattice

    Designed particle-interaction rules lead from computational models to an experimentally realized DNA-origami pyrochlore lattice, verified by scattering and microscopy.

    Science · 2024↗ — Inverse design of a pyrochlore lattice of DNA origami through model-driven experiments (opens in a new tab)

    First author

    Three crystal lattices from one scaffold

Molecular programming foundations

Work on RNA and RNA:DNA strand displacement tested how toehold length, substrate-end placement, and mismatch position affect reaction kinetics. It established an experimental foundation for reasoning about sequence, dynamics, and off-target behavior.

I also contributed to a localized DNA reaction network that implemented state transitions with temporal resolution.

ACS Synthetic Biology · 2021 — Kinetics of RNA and RNA:DNA Hybrid Strand Displacement (opens in a new tab)Science Advances · 2022 — A localized DNA finite-state machine with temporal resolution (opens in a new tab)

Related collaborations: hierarchical meta-DNA assembly (opens in a new tab) and conformational change in DNA nanostructures (opens in a new tab).

Toward functional materials

I want to make programmable assembly more general and predictable: specify a structure, encode its interactions, test competing outcomes, and build the resulting design.

Longer term, I am interested in positioning functional components and transferring DNA-designed geometry into durable materials, connecting precise nanoscale structure with optical and transport behavior.
